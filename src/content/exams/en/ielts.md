overview:
  - 'IELTS is managed jointly by the British Council, IDP Education and Cambridge University Press & Assessment, and is one of the most widely used English proficiency tests internationally.'
  - 'It is offered in two versions: Academic and General Training. Higher education applications generally require the Academic version, while migration and professional purposes may require General Training.'
  - 'IELTS assesses four skills — listening, reading, writing and speaking — reported as bands from 1 to 9, alongside an overall band.'
  - 'IELTS is not an academic qualification and does not assess subject knowledge. It measures the language ability needed to study, work and live in an English-speaking environment.'

  - 'Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking are each reported on a 1 to 9 band; the overall band is their mean, rounded to the nearest half band.'

  - q: 'What is the difference between Academic and General Training?'
    a: 'Academic is designed for higher education applications, with reading and writing tasks set in academic contexts. General Training is aimed at migration and professional purposes, with content closer to everyday and workplace situations.'
  - q: 'How long is an IELTS result valid?'
