The Hong Kong Diploma of Secondary Education is the public examination and leaving qualification covering the three senior-secondary years. Understanding its structure is more useful than memorising scattered exam tips.

## The three-year framework

The DSE spans three academic years. Across those years students progress through core subjects, the citizenship subject and their electives, ending in a public examination.

The curriculum framework is set by the Education Bureau, while the examinations are administered by the Hong Kong Examinations and Assessment Authority. **Curriculum authority and examining authority are separate bodies**, which is worth remembering when consulting official material.

## Core subjects: the part that cannot be substituted

Every candidate studies:

- Chinese Language
- English Language
- Mathematics
- Citizenship and Social Development

The first three provide the language and mathematics foundation; Citizenship and Social Development covers civic and social themes. Note that **from the 2024 DSE, Citizenship and Social Development replaced Liberal Studies**, and is reported as Attained / Not Attained rather than graded.

The purpose of the core is to guarantee a shared knowledge base across all DSE candidates — one reason DSE results convert relatively clearly for institutions.

## Electives: three sources

Electives come from three categories:

- **Category A — academic electives.** Common subjects including Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Economics, History, Geography and Information and Communication Technology.
- **Category B — Applied Learning.** Vocational and applied learning courses, typically reported as Attained or graded.
- **Category C — other languages.** French, German, Japanese, Spanish and others.

A common arrangement is two or three electives; the actual number and combination depend on what the school offers.

## Assessment: examinations and school-based assessment

The DSE is assessed mainly by public examination. Most subjects are divided into papers assessing different levels of ability.

Some subjects include **School-based Assessment (SBA)**, marked by school teachers and counted towards the final result. Its weighting and format vary by subject.

The practical implication: although the DSE is examination-led, it is not decided by a single sitting alone. In subjects with SBA, consistent performance during the course genuinely affects the final grade.

## How results are reported

Results are reported as levels:

- Levels 1 to 5 form the basic scale.
- Above Level 5, **5\*** and **5\*\*** distinguish the top, with 5\*\* the highest.
- Citizenship and Social Development is reported as **Attained / Not Attained**.

Because each subject is graded independently, a result sheet is a set of parallel subject levels rather than one aggregate score.

## Pathways

The most direct route is to Hong Kong universities. Local institutions admit local students mainly through the **Joint University Programmes Admissions System (JUPAS)**, for which DSE results are the most significant basis.

The DSE is also considered by some institutions overseas, including in the UK, Australia and Canada. Conversion methods and subject requirements differ by institution.

## Boundaries worth noting

- This platform does not publish specific admission scores or scoring formulas. These change annually and must be confirmed with institutions and official sources.
- The weighting and format of School-based Assessment can change by subject and by year.
- The minimum subject entry requirement, and the range of subjects a school can offer, depend on individual school arrangements.
