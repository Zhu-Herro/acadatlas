The hard part of preparing for the DSE is usually not that one subject is exceptionally difficult. It is **structural**: four core subjects plus two or three electives have to be sustained for three years, school-based assessment has to be completed along the way, and the public examinations come at the end of it.

So the first useful decision is not "how many hours a day" — it is getting the three-year rhythm right.

## Two parallel tracks, not one

DSE assessment runs on two tracks: **public examinations** and **School-based Assessment (SBA)**. Most subjects are assessed mainly by examination; some include SBA.

The two tracks are distributed very differently in time:

- **SBA** is spread across Secondary 5 and 6, marked by school teachers, and cannot be made up at the end.
- **Public examinations** sit at the end of Secondary 6 and can be approached with a concentrated final push.

That produces a common misjudgement: **putting all the weight on the final sprint**. SBA finishes before the sprint begins, so if the earlier years were treated as slack, there is nothing left to recover with.

## Four stages

The staging below is a **structural suggestion** derived from how DSE assessment is distributed in time, not a description of any particular school's timetable. Follow your school's schedule where it differs.

### Stage 1 — Secondary 4: build the floor

The job here is not exam technique. It is **flattening out the basics of every subject**.

- Identify, in each core subject (Chinese Language, English Language, Mathematics), where the weak points actually are.
- Confirm that the elective choices are right. Once Secondary 5 starts, changing subject costs considerably more.
- Citizenship and Social Development accumulates from here. It is reported as Attained / Not Attained, so it must be completed but does not need chasing for a grade.

**The most common error at this stage**: treating Secondary 4 as "there is still time". Elective content is substantial; without a floor built in S4, S5 carries new material and revision at the same time.

### Stage 2 — Secondary 5: SBA begins

S5 is when **school-based assessment actually happens**, and that is the main difference from S4.

- In subjects with SBA, marks feed into the final result, so performance here has long-term consequences.
- Start organising notes and error logs systematically rather than waiting for S6.
- In English, work out now how far you are from any language requirement your target institutions may have.

### Stage 3 — Secondary 6, first term: narrow and patch

The strategy shifts from spreading out to **narrowing in**.

- For each subject, identify the actual failure mode: unclear concepts, unfamiliar question types, or running out of time. The three have completely different remedies.
- Complete any remaining SBA requirements.
- If an additional language test is needed, this is the realistic window for scheduling it.

### Stage 4 — Secondary 6, second term: simulate and stabilise

- The aim is **consistency**, not further expansion of content.
- Replace chapter-by-chapter practice with full timed papers, because DSE losses often come from time allocation rather than missing knowledge.
- Adjust your daily rhythm so you are at your best during the actual examination slots.

## Three things that need an early start

These are **structural judgements**: their lead times are longer than most people assume, and the cost of starting late is high.

1. **English Language.** Language ability improves over years, not months. If target institutions have a language requirement, planning it in S6 leaves very little room.
2. **SBA project work.** It runs on the school's fixed timetable and cannot be accelerated individually.
3. **Confirming the elective direction.** If an elective turns out to be misaligned with the intended field, the earlier it is corrected the cheaper it is.

## Three scheduling mistakes

**Mistake one: spreading revision evenly across three years.**
The right shape is a **lighter load early and heavier later — but with higher quality demands early**. S4 and S5 are for depth of understanding; S6 is for exam intensity.

**Mistake two: underestimating the long-term effect of SBA.**
In subjects with SBA, ongoing performance genuinely enters the final result and cannot be compensated for later.

**Mistake three: substituting chapter practice for full-paper practice.**
Chapter practice answers "do I know it". Full papers answer "can I finish in time". The second is consistently underweighted in how students prepare.

## One reminder about grades

DSE subjects are graded independently, from Level 1 to Level 5, with 5\* and 5\*\* above Level 5; Citizenship and Social Development is reported as Attained / Not Attained.

There is therefore **no official concept of a "total DSE score"**. Institutions and programmes define their own scoring and subject weighting, so before setting a target, confirm the actual scoring rules of the programmes you are aiming at.
