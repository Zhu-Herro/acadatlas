overview:
  - 'The International General Certificate of Secondary Education (IGCSE) is an international qualification for students aged roughly 14 to 16. It corresponds to the UK GCSE but is designed for international school contexts.'
  - 'IGCSE is not itself a university entrance qualification. Its function within an education pathway is articulation: after IGCSE, students normally move into A-Level, the IB Diploma Programme or another post-16 course.'
  - 'Because IGCSE allows students to study a comparatively large number of subjects at once, it is often the stage at which subject breadth is maintained before specialisation begins.'
  - 'Different awarding bodies offer IGCSE with differing subject lists, grading scales and assessment arrangements. Schools choose according to their own curriculum design.'

faq:
  - q: 'What is the difference between IGCSE and GCSE?'
    a: 'They sit at roughly the same academic level. GCSE is used mainly within the UK, while IGCSE is designed for international school settings and adapts subject provision and assessment accordingly.'
  - q: 'Can IGCSE be used to apply to university directly?'
    a: 'Not normally on its own. Students usually continue into A-Level, the IB or another post-16 course and apply using results from that stage.'
  - q: 'How many IGCSE subjects should be taken?'
    a: 'A common range is five to ten or more, depending on the school\u2019s curriculum and the student\u2019s subsequent plans.'
  - q: 'What is the difference between A*–G and 9–1 grading?'
    a: 'They are different reporting scales. The traditional scale runs from A* to G; the newer scale runs from 9 to 1, with 9 as the highest. Which applies depends on the awarding body and region.'
