overview:
  - 'The GCE Advanced Level (A-Level) is a post-16 qualification in the UK, usually completed over two years of sixth-form study, and is the main academic basis for UK university applications.'
  - 'Its defining characteristic is "fewer subjects, greater depth": students typically take three or four subjects, but each is studied to a demanding standard. This contrasts clearly with the IB, which requires coverage across several subject groups.'
  - 'Outside the UK, international schools commonly use A-Level courses offered by Cambridge Assessment International Education or Pearson Edexcel. Examination arrangements differ slightly from the UK domestic versions.'
  - 'Several awarding bodies in the UK can issue A-Level qualifications, and schools choose which to use. Students at different schools within the same country may therefore sit examinations set by different boards.'

faq:
  - q: 'How should a student choose between A-Level and the IB?'
    a: 'If the subject direction is already clear and depth in a few subjects is preferred, A-Level is usually a better structural fit. If breadth across disciplines and multi-country applications matter more, the IB may suit better. School provision and individual capacity should also be weighed.'
  - q: 'Three subjects or four?'
    a: 'Three is a common minimum, while four is often used to strengthen a particular direction. Whether four is needed depends on the requirements of the target institutions and programmes.'
  - q: 'Do AS results still matter?'
    a: 'An AS result can evidence progress at a point in time, but UK undergraduate admission is normally based on the full A-Level results. How much weight institutions give to AS varies.'
  - q: 'Are A-Levels from different boards equivalent?'
    a: 'Qualifications from all the main boards are recognised by UK universities, but paper structure, assessment weighting and subject emphasis can differ. Schools normally choose a board to match their teaching arrangements.'
