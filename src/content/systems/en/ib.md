overview:
  - 'The International Baccalaureate (IB) is offered by the International Baccalaureate Organization (IBO) as a complete continuum of programmes spanning several age ranges, rather than a one-off examination.'
  - 'The continuum typically includes the Primary Years Programme (PYP), the Middle Years Programme (MYP), and the Diploma Programme (DP) and Career-related Programme (CP) for ages 16–19. The Diploma Programme is the one most directly relevant to university admission.'
  - 'The DP is structured around six subject groups and three core elements: the Extended Essay, Theory of Knowledge, and Creativity, Activity, Service. Those core elements are the clearest structural distinction between the IB and other qualifications.'
  - 'The DP combines internal assessment with externally set examinations, and both the curriculum and the assessment standards are defined centrally by the IBO. IB schools in different countries therefore operate with a high degree of consistency.'

  - 'There are also completion requirements attached to the core; an incomplete core affects whether the diploma is awarded.'
  - 'Assessment standards are defined by the IBO, so the same framework is applied worldwide.'
